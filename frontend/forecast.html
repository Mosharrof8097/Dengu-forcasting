Forecast evaluation

Every forecast on this page was made before its outcome was known, and is shown against what actually happened.

These are rolling-origin forecasts: at each origin the model saw only data up to that date. Nothing is a live prediction of an unknown future, so you can judge the forecaster against the record rather than take its word.

What this model is for. On this panel the weekly lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.973 — last week's count is an extremely strong guess at this week's. The model does not reliably beat that guess on point accuracy: across six folds its median is slightly worse at t+1, slightly better at t+2 and slightly worse at t+4, which is a tie. The figures above say so rather than hide it. What it does do better, by roughly a quarter on the weighted interval score at every horizon, is state how uncertain it is — and for a capacity decision the width of the interval is the part you act on.

How this evaluation was run

Rolling origin, with an embargo. The record is split into six folds by time. For each, the model trains on everything before the origin and is scored on what follows, with a gap between them so that no feature computed from a rolling window can see across the boundary. Nothing is ever scored on data it trained on.

The folds are chosen to be hard. They deliberately straddle changes of epidemic phase — a quiet year followed by a growth year, a growth phase followed by a peak. A random split would score far better and would mean nothing, because the use case is precisely to forecast across a change of phase.

Intervals are conformalised (Romano et al., conformalised quantile regression). The raw quantile fits covered only 74.6% at a nominal 90%. Calibration uses the most recent 20% of each training window, so the correction is estimated on the period most like the one being predicted.

WIS — the weighted interval score — scores the whole predictive distribution, penalising both a wrong centre and an interval that is wider than it needs to be. It is the standard metric in epidemic forecast evaluation (it is what the COVID-19 Forecast Hub used), which is why the headline comparison is stated in it rather than in error alone.

Persistence+ is the honest baseline: last observed value as the point forecast, with an interval from the historical spread of week-on-week changes. It is reported because on a panel this autocorrelated, a model that cannot beat it is not useful, and that comparison should be in front of the reader rather than in an appendix.

Coverage below nominal is reported, not corrected away. Where the 90% interval covers less than 90% of outcomes, capacity sized from it is too small — an error in the dangerous direction. The fold table above shows exactly where that happens.

Source: DGHS daily dengue press releases rebuilt at 64-district daily resolution, aggregated to complete weeks. Partial weeks are excluded from evaluation; only weeks with all seven days reported are used.
