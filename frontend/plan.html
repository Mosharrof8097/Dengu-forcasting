This is a historical distribution, not a forecast. It says what this district has needed before, which is the right starting point for a standing capacity decision. It is not a statement about the coming season — if an epidemic exceeds anything in the record, so will the requirement. For a forward-looking figure see the Forecast tab, and read the coverage figures there before using it.

How this number was derived

1. Occupancy comes from the bulletins, not from cases. Each DGHS bulletin prints the number of patients currently admitted per district. That column is bed demand; nothing needs to be inferred from case counts. The service level you pick selects a quantile of those observed daily values.

2. The record is internally consistent. The identity occupancy[t] = occupancy[t−1] + admissions − discharges − deaths reproduces the printed stock exactly on 99.3% of 106,179 consecutive day-pairs. The occupancy column is therefore not a separate, possibly inconsistent series — it is tied to the flows.

3. Length of stay is measured, not assumed. By Little's Law, LOS = occupancy ÷ admission rate. It is computed only on days with at least 5 admissions, because the ratio is meaningless when the denominator is one or two patients, and the median is reported because that resists the remaining small denominators. It is shown here for interpretation; the bed figure does not depend on it, since occupancy is observed directly.

4. Season matters more than the annual average. Dengue in Bangladesh is concentrated in July–November, and the seasonal and all-year distributions differ by close to an order of magnitude. Sizing on the annual figure under-provisions the season; the seasonal view is the default for that reason.

5. What a service level means. At 95% the capacity you set is enough on 95% of days in the historical distribution and short on the other 5%. It is not a safety guarantee, and the right level depends on what being short costs you against what idle capacity costs you — a judgement no dataset can make.

What is deliberately absent. Earlier versions of this app multiplied peak cases by 3.0 for beds, 1.8 for test kits and 2.5 for saline. Those constants had no source. Beds are now read from the record. Kits and saline are not shown at all, because the bulletins contain no consumable data and a number with no provenance is worse than a blank.

Source: DGHS daily dengue press releases. 1,674 bulletin PDFs covering 1,669 distinct dates, 2019-08-27 to 2026-07-07, rebuilt at 64-district daily resolution.
